Frequently Asked Questions

Everything a parent or homeschool family needs to know before enrolling — from how SUFS funding works to what a typical Saturday looks like at X39.

Getting Started
What is Mission Aviation Academy?
Mission Aviation Academy (MAA) is the academic division of Infinity Aero Club Tampa Bay, Inc. — a Florida 501(c)(3) nonprofit operating at Tampa North Aero Park (X39) in Lutz, FL. We deliver FAA-credentialed aviation, drone, STEM, entrepreneurship, and AI programs for students ages 9 through adult. Every student enters through an individualized orientation, skills assessment, and personal training roadmap — we do not run generic classes. We are a registered SUFS provider, SAM.gov active, and EIN 85-1110607.
My child attends a private school or homeschool co-op. Can they enroll?
Yes — and enrollment is the same regardless of school type. Private school students, homeschoolers, and co-op students all follow the same entry process: register and complete onboarding, take an initial assessment, receive a personal training roadmap, and begin their first session. If your school has a partnership MOU with MAA, your cohort may have dedicated session blocks and scholarship-funded seats available. Contact us to check whether your school is a current partner.
What is the first step to enroll?
The first step is the Registration & Onboarding ($200, one-time). This covers your student's individual orientation, account setup, skills assessment, and a personalized training blueprint built around their goals — approximately two hours of direct staff time. It is required before any course begins and is SUFS-fundable. After onboarding, your student selects their first course or subscription tier and books their first session.
What ages do you serve?
We serve students ages 9 through adult. Age-based track recommendations:
  • Ages 9–12: Tier 1–2 enrichment, morning Saturday sessions — drone discovery, simulator intro, STEM foundations
  • Ages 13–15: Tier 2–3, afternoon Saturday sessions — drone certification pathway begins, AI Innovation
  • Ages 16–17: Tier 3–4, certification focus — FAA Part 107 eligibility, Part 107 Phase 1 ($800)
  • Ages 18+: Tier 4 and career track — full Part 107, flight simulator program, apprenticeship pathway
SUFS Funding
Can we use our Step Up For Students scholarship funds at MAA?
Yes. MAA is a registered SUFS provider and 38+ of our courses and programs are live and bookable through the SUFS EMA marketplace. If your family holds an active FES-UA, FTC, or AAA scholarship balance, you can apply those funds directly toward MAA enrollment — including the one-time onboarding fee, monthly enrichment tiers, AI Innovation courses, drone certification, and the flight simulator program. Contact us or log into your SUFS account and search "Mission Aviation Academy" to apply funds directly.
What does SUFS funding actually cover?
SUFS funds can cover: Registration & Onboarding ($200), Monthly Enrichment Tier subscriptions ($99–$249/mo), Academic Tutoring sessions ($90–$100/hr), AI Innovation courses ($450–$825), Drone & Aerospace STEM courses ($450 each), FAA Part 107 Phase 1 Foundations ($800), and the Flight Simulator Program ($3,395). Ground school, solo flight training, workforce programs, and camps are available but not yet SUFS-listed — contact us for those options directly.
Our SUFS balance is limited. What's the most strategic way to use it?
Start with Registration & Onboarding ($200) — required and SUFS-fundable. Then, depending on your student's age and goals: for ages 9–15, a Tier 2 or Tier 3 monthly subscription ($149–$199/mo) gives the broadest course access. For ages 16+, the Part 107 Phase 1 course ($800) is the highest-credential, highest-career-value use of a larger balance. For families with limited balances, à la carte sessions at $99/individual or $49/person in a group are available without a subscription commitment.

Credentials & Outcomes
What credentials can my child earn?
Credentials available through MAA programs:
  • FAA TRUST Certificate — Recreational drone operator (ages 12+, Part I Drone course)
  • FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate — Commercial drone pilot (ages 16+, Part 107 Phase 1–3)
  • Drone Business Certificates — 8 specializations including mapping, inspection, agriculture (ages 16+)
  • Flight Simulator Completion Certificate — 26 sessions on FAA-approved simulator with CFI (ages 12+)
  • AI Innovation Certificates — Parts 1 and 2, app-building with AI tools (all ages)
  • MAA Internship Certificate — For students completing the MAA Internship Program or equivalent tracks
Every student receives a portfolio, logbook entries, and letters of recommendation documenting their training pathway.
Are your instructors background-checked?
Yes. Background checks are a condition of any student-contact role at MAA — for lead instructors, drone instructors, simulator instructors, and any staff or volunteer with unsupervised student access. Documentation is available to school partners upon request as part of the MOU process.
For School Administrators & Principals

Partnership Process & Requirements

What school administrators need to know before signing a partnership MOU — compliance, curriculum, cohort structure, and the School Partner Scholarship Fund.

What does a school partnership with MAA actually look like?
A school partnership begins with a Tier 1 Exploratory Partnership MOU — a non-binding framework agreement that establishes intent and the addendum structure. No financial commitments are made at this stage. From there, a Tier 2 School Cohort Addendum names the specific student cohort, credentials, schedule, location, cost, funding source, and outcome reporting requirements. The MOU and addendum together define the working relationship; each addendum covers a specific program area (drone, internships, STEM lab, field trips, fundraising, books, gala).
What does MAA require before the MOU can advance?
Three items are required before a school MOU can progress beyond the exploratory stage:
  • Background checks — completed for all MAA instructors with student contact (online or in-person). Documentation provided to the school upon request.
  • Cohort Addendum terms agreed — specific program, credential, schedule, location, and funding source named in writing.
  • No-duplication-of-payment budget — a written per-student budget identifying every funding source (SUFS, WIA, grant, sponsor, school, family) so no expense is billed twice.
What curriculum does MAA actually deliver — and does MAA create it?
MAA creates and delivers its own aviation, drone, STEM, entrepreneurship, and AI curriculum — we are not a broker. Lead instruction is provided by Ricardo E. Foster (LCDR, USN Ret., FAA Remote Pilot, LSRM certificate holder) as primary instructor. Drone practicals are delivered by FAA-credentialed drone instructors. Flight simulator sessions are conducted with a CFI (Phillip Mednick, FAA Cert. No. 3205411, Exp. April 2028). Curriculum scope and sequence, instructor qualifications, and student-to-instructor ratios are available in the MAA Master Course Catalog (v2.6) — provided to school partners upon request.
What does FERPA compliance look like for school partner students?
MAA operates as a third-party service provider under FERPA. Student records generated by MAA (session logs, credential documentation, internship hours, assessment results) are shared with the partnering school as part of the cohort's outcome reporting requirements — named in the School Cohort Addendum. No student information is shared with third parties outside the MOU. Schools retain primary FERPA responsibility for their students; MAA complies with school-specific data handling requirements as documented in the addendum.
How does the School Partner Scholarship Fund work?
The MAA School Partner Scholarship Fund is a dedicated fundraising vehicle that covers the cost of program enrollment for school partner students — so the school pays nothing and families pay nothing out-of-pocket beyond what their SUFS balance covers. Each school partner receives a named cohort fund (e.g., "[Your School Name] Student Access Fund"). Donors contribute to that specific fund; contributions are tracked and reported to the school. The fund is activated through the MOU and managed by MAA's development team. Each school's fund has a designated Campaign Chair responsible for donor cultivation, the I Fly student storytelling campaign, bulk book outreach, and the annual gala at X39.
Our families cannot redirect SUFS balances to MAA. Does that block the partnership?
No. The School Partner Scholarship Fund was specifically designed for this situation. When SUFS cross-funding is not available at the school level, the scholarship fund covers the enrollment cost directly — students receive the same program at no out-of-pocket cost to the family. The fund is seeded through employer sponsors, individual donors, bulk book purchases, and the annual joint fundraising gala. Contact us to discuss the fund structure and the initial fundraising target for your school cohort.
What outcome data does MAA provide to school partners?
MAA provides the following outcome data to school partners as part of the Cohort Addendum reporting requirements: session attendance records, credentials earned per student, internship hours logged, employer interviews completed, simulator hours, assessment scores, and letters of recommendation issued. Reporting cadence (monthly, quarterly, or semester) is negotiated in the addendum. All data is provided in a format compatible with the school's existing reporting systems.
